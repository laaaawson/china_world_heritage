## The Past (I): The Testament of the Tea Ancestor

The Bulang tell how their ancestor Pa Ai Leng led the tribe to Jingmai Mountain, taught the people to clear the slopes and plant tea, and left this testament: "Gold and silver you may leave behind, and in time they will run out; cattle and horses you may leave behind, and in time they will die; but leave the tea seed, and your descendants will draw on it endlessly, and it will never fail." From that day tea became the root of the Bulang people's very existence.

## The Past (II): The Wisdom of Growing Tea Under the Forest

The old tea forests of Jingmai are not pruned or fertilised. Canopy trees, tea trees and herbs grow in three storeys: tall evergreen trees shade the tea, the tea grows naturally "under the forest," and herbs and moss hold the soil and water. This almost primordial style of understorey tea farming has let tea and forest survive together for over a thousand years. The thousand-year-old, ten-thousand-mu ancient tea forest surviving on Jingmai is among the best-preserved old tea forests in the world.

## The Present: The World's First Tea-Themed World Heritage

In 2023, the Cultural Landscape of Old Tea Forests of the Jingmai Mountain in Pu'er was inscribed under criteria (iii) and (v) — the world's first World Heritage property themed on tea culture. The villages of the Bulang and Dai peoples, the old tea forests and the worship of the tea ancestor together form a living heritage in which "the mountain shelters the forest, the forest grows the tea, and the tea nourishes the people."

## Outstanding Universal Value (OUV)

- **Criterion (iii)**: The old tea forests bear witness to the continuity of a thousand-year tea culture among the Bulang, Dai and other peoples.
- **Criterion (v)**: The distinctive understorey tea-growing land use is an outstanding example of harmonious coexistence between mountain peoples and forest.
