## The Past (I): The Hakka "Castles"

From the Western Jin onward, migrants from the Central Plains moved south into Fujian and merged with local peoples to become the Hakka. To fend off bandits, wild beasts and clan feuds, the Hakka rammed raw earth into the outer walls of vast round or square buildings: walls more than a metre thick, no windows at ground level — once the gate closed, the lives and property of a whole clan were sheltered.

## The Past (II): A Rammed-Earth Wonder from Song-Yuan to the Republic

Tulou building began in the Song and Yuan and flourished in the Ming and Qing. Yongding's Chengqi Lou, over seventy metres across, with four concentric rings and more than three hundred rooms, is called the "King of Tulou"; the five buildings of Tianluokeng in Nanjing — "four dishes and a soup" — are the classic image of a tulou cluster; Hua'an's Eryi Lou is famed for its elaborate defensive system. Rammed-earth walls have stood through centuries of wind and rain — a "wonder of the world's vernacular architecture."

## The Present: World Heritage and Living On

In 2008, the Fujian Tulou were inscribed on the World Heritage List under criteria (iii), (iv) and (v), covering 46 representative buildings in Yongding, Nanjing and Hua'an counties. Today many tulou remain homes: when cooking smoke rises, the sound of water in the well and the talk under the eaves keep this living heritage continuing its eight hundred years of everyday life.

## Outstanding Universal Value (OUV)

- **Criterion (iii)**: The tulou are a unique testimony to the migration, settlement and mutual-aid traditions of the Hakka people.
- **Criterion (iv)**: In technique, form and settlement layout, the giant rammed-earth buildings are outstanding examples of architecture.
- **Criterion (v)**: The settlement system of tulou, terraces and woodland is an excellent example of human interaction with the environment.

## Facts at a Glance

- 46 representative tulou, inscribed in 2008
- Chengqi Lou is some 70 m across — the "King of Tulou"; the largest round buildings are all protected monuments
