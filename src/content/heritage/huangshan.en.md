## The Past (I): Strange Peaks and Seas of Cloud

Huangshan has seventy-two peaks; Lotus Peak, Bright Summit and Celestial Capital stand as its tripod, with Lotus Peak — the highest, at 1,864.8 metres — in the lead. Granite carved by glaciers and running water over hundreds of millions of years has become the "four wonders": odd pines, strange rocks, cloud seas and hot springs. The Greeting Pine reaches out an arm to welcome every visitor; rocks like "Monkey Watching the Sea" and "Dreaming Pen Blooms a Flower" are uncannily lifelike; and when the clouds surge, the peaks float like immortal mountains above the sea.

## The Past (II): Mountain of Immortals, Mountain of Painters

Legend says the Yellow Emperor once refined his elixir in these mountains and ascended to heaven — hence the name Huangshan ("Yellow Mountain"). In the late Ming and early Qing, painters such as Jian Jiang, Mei Qing and Shi Tao lived here for long periods sketching, founding the "Huangshan school" and writing its pines, rocks and cloud seas into the history of Chinese art. Xu Xiake climbed the mountain twice and declared: "Of all the famous mountains within and beyond the seas, none can match Huangshan of Huizhou."

## The Present: The Road to a Double Heritage

In 1990, Mount Huangshan was inscribed under criteria (ii), (vii) and (x) as a mixed cultural and natural heritage; in 2004 it was also named a UNESCO Global Geopark. Today the fame of "having seen Huangshan, no sacred mountain can impress" — together with the resilient spirit of the Huangshan pine — has long been a symbol of Chinese mountain culture.

## Outstanding Universal Value (OUV)

- **Criterion (ii)**: Artistic traditions inspired by Huangshan, such as the Huangshan school of painting, profoundly influenced Chinese landscape painting and garden art.
- **Criterion (vii)**: The landscape of pines, rocks, cloud seas and springs is the supreme expression of natural beauty in Chinese mountains.
- **Criterion (x)**: Huangshan preserves rich vertical biodiversity and is a habitat for many rare species.
