## The Past (I): The Pristine Realm of the Roof of Central China

Shennongjia lies in western Hubei; its main peak, Shennong Peak, rises 3,106.2 metres — the highest in central China. It preserves the most complete primeval forest at mid-latitudes in the Northern Hemisphere: fir forests, arrow bamboo groves and alpine meadows unfold tier by tier with altitude, and it is China's only county-level administrative unit named a "forest district." The Quaternary glaciations once swept across Eurasia, yet Shennongjia, by the accident of its terrain, became a "refuge" for many relict species.

## The Past (II): Wild Men, Herbs and Legends

Legend says Shennong "erected ladders to gather herbs and tasted every plant" here — that is how the mountain got its name; the folk mystery of the "wild man" has cast an air of the unknown over these primeval forests. The old salt road between Sichuan and Hubei once crossed the mountains, and the porters' work songs and herb-gathering chants echoed through the gorges — the coexistence of people and wilderness has continued here for millennia.

## The Present: From Expeditions to World Heritage

Since the 1970s, Chinese and foreign expeditions have repeatedly penetrated Shennongjia, discovering great numbers of rare flora and fauna: the Sichuan golden snub-nosed monkey, the dove tree, the Chinese yew and others live and grow here. In 1986, the Shennongjia National Nature Reserve was established; in 2016, Hubei Shennongjia was inscribed on the World Heritage List under criteria (ix)(x). Today, above the sea of clouds at Shennong Peak, the calls of the golden snub-nosed monkeys still echo through this "green treasury" of central China.

## Outstanding Universal Value (OUV)

- **Criterion (ix)**: Shennongjia preserves a complete subtropical montane vertical ecosystem, recording the ongoing processes of biological evolution.
- **Criterion (x)**: As a refuge for relict species, Shennongjia is an important habitat for the dove tree, the Sichuan golden snub-nosed monkey and other rare species.

## Facts at a Glance

- Main peak Shennong at 3,106.2 m — the highest in central China
- Inscribed in 2016; preserves the most complete mid-latitude primeval forest in the Northern Hemisphere
